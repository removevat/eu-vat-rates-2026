How the maths works in every country

The arithmetic is identical everywhere: to add VAT, multiply the net price by 1 plus the rate (net × 1.20 at 20%, net × 1.19 at 19%, net × 1.21 at 21%). To remove VAT from a gross price — the vat conversion people get wrong most often — divide by the same figure (gross ÷ 1.20, ÷ 1.19, ÷ 1.21). Never subtract the percentage from a gross price: at 20%, £120 minus 20% is £96, but the right net is £100, because the VAT was calculated on the smaller net figure. The same trap exists in every language: 119 € minus 19% is not 100 €, and 121 € minus 21% is not 100 € either.

What changes across borders

The rate is only half the question. Selling goods to a customer in another EU country can be zero-rated if the customer is VAT registered; selling digital services usually means charging the VAT rate of the customer's country, not yours. The UK's £90,000 registration threshold has no EU equivalent — each member state sets its own. Keep the rate table above for the arithmetic, and check the place-of-supply rules for the treatment.
